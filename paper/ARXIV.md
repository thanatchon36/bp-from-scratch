# arXiv submission

The LaTeX source is arXiv-ready: it compiles with pdfLaTeX from TeX Live (the engine arXiv uses) without
errors or undefined references, and the bibliography is inline, so no `.bib` or `.bbl` file is needed.
Every reference was checked against Crossref (DOI) or the arXiv API and carries its DOI or arXiv identifier.

## Build the upload

```bash
make arxiv     # dist/arxiv/bp-from-scratch-arxiv.tar.gz, plus a pdfLaTeX test build when Docker is available
```

Upload the tarball under "Upload files" (arXiv unpacks it and finds `main.tex`).

**Abstract** (the arXiv form allows at most 1,920 characters; this condensed version of the paper's abstract has
1,876 and uses arXiv's TeX notation):

```text
Barren plateaus, gradients whose variance vanishes exponentially in the number of qubits, are the central obstacle to training variational quantum circuits. We present a small, fully auditable NumPy state-vector simulator (about 500 lines, exact adjoint gradients, validated against independent references and PennyLane) and use it to reproduce and extend the main barren-plateau results on 2-16 qubits. We derive closed-form Haar (unitary 2-design) values for the gradient variance with the random circuit before and/or after a rotation and verify them numerically. A hardware-efficient ansatz approaches the 2-design value non-monotonically with depth; its gradient distribution is strongly heavy-tailed at shallow depth (kurtosis up to ~80) and becomes Gaussian only where the variance saturates; and the variance saturates while the half-chain entanglement entropy is still below half of the Page value. We confirm the cost-function dependence of Cerezo et al. (global costs decay as $4^{-n}$, local costs much more slowly), compare small-angle and identity-block initialisations, and show that with exact gradients Adam prepares $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ through a global cost even at $n=14$, where $\|\nabla C\|\approx 10^{-4}$, whereas with $10^4$-shot gradient noise it fails progressively beyond $n=10$ while the local cost keeps succeeding. QAOA for MaxCut on random 3-regular graphs shows no plateau at fixed depth. Finally, we compute dynamical Lie algebras exactly for four ansatz families and test the Lie-algebraic prediction $\mathrm{Var}[C]=\mathcal{P}_\mathfrak{g}(\rho)\mathcal{P}_\mathfrak{g}(H)/\dim\mathfrak{g}$ of Ragone et al.: it holds to within 10% for the three families that reach the 2-design regime, including a hardware-efficient family whose algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(2n)$, of dimension $n(2n-1)$. Every figure is regenerated by a single script.
```
